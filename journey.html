01

The spark

It started with one class in my last year of high school.

2020 – 2021

AP Computer Science, senior year

High school · graduated 2021
Coding

I took AP Computer Science my senior year, and it was the first time code felt like something I could make things with. We got into game development: movement, collisions, making a character feel right when it jumps.

For my final project I built my own version of Doodle Jump with Expo. It was rough around the edges, but watching my friends play something I'd made got me hooked.

02

The kitchen

After graduating I wasn't sure college was the right next step yet, so I got a job in a kitchen. It shaped me more than I expected.

2021 – 2023

Champion's Curry

Irvine, CA · eventually assistant kitchen manager
Work

My first job was at a small curry shop in a food court, with a team of four. A team that small gets close fast. They taught me how to hold a knife, dice an onion, fillet a salmon, and keep a station organized so service goes smoothly. I loved seeing my chives get cut a little finer each day.

My manager there did a lot more for me than teach me to cook. They taught me how to drive, gave me rides to work, and bought me my first beer. By the time I left I was helping train new staff and run daily operations, but what I remember most is the people.

What stuck: you get better by showing up and doing the small things a little better each day.
Around then

I almost went to culinary school

A fork in the road

I loved the kitchen enough that I seriously considered culinary school. But I kept thinking about how it felt to build that game in high school, and I wasn't ready to let that go.

03

Learning to code, between shifts

I kept cooking, but I didn't want to give up on becoming a developer.

While still cooking

UCI Coding Bootcamp, full-stack web development

Irvine, CA · 6 months
CodingStill on the line

While I was still working the line, I enrolled in UC Irvine's coding bootcamp and tried to learn as much about web development as I could, especially the MERN stack: MongoDB, Express, React, and Node.

I built my first real, deployable projects there, with a team. I learned how to work in agile sprints, design in Figma before writing code, collaborate through GitHub, and debug without panicking.

What stuck: software is a team sport, a lot like a kitchen during a dinner rush.
After the bootcamp

The job search that didn't work out

I graduated into one of the toughest years for junior developers in a long time. I applied and applied and didn't land anything. It was discouraging.

Instead of giving up, I decided to go back to school full time. This time I would go all in on computer science and build a real foundation.

What stuck: a "no" isn't the end. Sometimes it means you need to build a stronger foundation first.
04

All in at Irvine Valley College

It took three years to finish my transfer prerequisites. They were some of the busiest and best years I've had.

2022 – 2025

Computer science at Irvine Valley College

A.S. Computer Science · May 2025
School

This time I wanted the fundamentals the bootcamp skipped: object-oriented programming, data structures, discrete math, calculus, and linear algebra. Three years is longer than a lot of people spend at community college, but it gave me a foundation I lean on every day.

2023 – 2025

Wok chef at Din Tai Fung

Costa Mesa, CA
Work

I went back to the line to pay for school, this time at Din Tai Fung. The kitchen was a lot bigger and busier, in a good way. Almost everyone in the back of house spoke Spanish, so I picked up a lot of phrases (some good, some I won't repeat here) and made friends from all kinds of backgrounds.

2023 – 2024

Private math tutor

Irvine, CA
Teaching

I started tutoring algebra, geometry, and calculus for kids I met through community college. I found out I really enjoy teaching, especially the moment a student stops being scared of a problem.

2023 – 2024

Project director, IVC Computer Science Club

Irvine Valley College
CodingTeaching

I used what I learned at the bootcamp to run the club's workshops. I created my own workshop series on React, Git, and web development, ran more than 10 sessions, and helped students deploy the first real projects they'd ever shipped.

Along the way I learned how to plan events, make flyers people actually read, and find friends to go to hackathons with.

What stuck: explaining something is the best way to find out whether you really understand it.

Nov 2024

Winning at HackSC with EcoTracker

USC · 1st place, Adventure category
HackathonCoding

A few weeks later I tried again at HackSC, with a team of four. We built EcoTracker, a mobile app that turns recycling into a game with streaks and rankings, and uses the Gemini API to check photos so nobody can fake their eco-actions. This time we kept the scope small and made sure the demo worked, and we won our category.

Summer 2025

My first engineering internship, at Drysci

Irvine, CA · Software engineering intern
CodingWork

Years after that first job search, I finally got to write code for a real company. I worked on a 4-person team building internal tools that turn JSON templates into client websites. I wrote Python scripts that convert templates into SQL schema, built Laravel and Livewire form components, and tracked down bugs through every layer of the stack. Most of it was a framework I'd never used, so I spent a lot of time reading documentation.

05

UC San Diego

I transferred to study data science. UCSD's curriculum is what drew me in, and I wanted to go deeper into machine learning and AI.

2025 – now

B.S. Data Science

UC San Diego · expected March 2028
School

Here the math finally meets the code: probability, statistical methods, Bayesian theory, and the theory behind the models I used to just import.

I still work in restaurants: I bus tables at Katsuya in La Jolla on the side.

2025 – now

Event organizer, Skate Club at UCSD

La Jolla, CA
Community

Skating has always been how I meet people, so I joined the club and started organizing. I plan socials, house shows, and group sessions, and I teach beginners how to stand on a board. Skating brings together people from every background, and I want everyone who shows up to feel welcome.

2025 – 2026

Learning to ask better questions with data

Coding

My projects started asking questions instead of only building things. With a team of four, I studied whether colleges with more Pell Grant students graduate fewer STEM majors, using data from 1,600+ schools. The answer was "a little, but not by much." Learning to explain the difference between statistically significant and actually meaningful stuck with me.

Then I asked a question I'd argued about with friends for years: in League of Legends, can farming make up for losing early fights? After 18,000+ pro matches and a permutation test, mostly yes. Read the analysis ↗

2026 – now

Research: the Seibert Lab and the HDSI Scholars program

UC San Diego
Research

I'm an undergraduate research assistant in a cancer imaging lab. I align prostate MRI scans with pathology, and I wrote a Python script that checks and transfers terabytes of patient imaging data to collaborators at Stanford, work that used to be done by hand.

As an HDSI Undergraduate Scholar, I'm working with the City of San Diego, Data Science Alliance, and the Lucky Duck Foundation to understand what drives people into homelessness, using unemployment, housing, and shelter data. It's the first time my work could directly help people in my own city.

3-person hackathon team
